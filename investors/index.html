01 / The opportunity

Small businesses need a guest layer, not another system migration.

The commercial hypothesis: capture more interactions around menus and services with minimal setup, while leaving existing operational software in place.

01 / Existing offer

The merchant already has a menu.

Digitising it should not demand a custom website, a new till or a lengthy implementation project.

02 / Guest action

One link or QR code.

A browser storefront can bring browsing, demo orders, tips and feedback into one mobile interaction.

03 / Merchant insight

Every interaction creates a signal.

Usage and feedback could make the service valuable beyond setup — if pilots show sustained adoption.

03 / Evidence, without inflated traction

What works today — and what remains unproven.

Working software is not yet product-market fit. This is what the browser testing supports, and what merchant pilots need to establish next.

Verified in browser QA

A functional service workflow

  • Merchant access and workspace
  • Guest QR menu in a browser
  • Demo order creation and status progression
  • Tip and feedback interactions
  • Merchant-side reporting updates

QA evidence dated 4 August 2026. Screens and sample amounts shown on this page use demo data.

Still needs commercial evidence

The investment milestones

  • Production payment settlement and reconciliation
  • Signed or paying pilot merchants
  • Willingness to pay at each proposed tier
  • Retention, customer acquisition and margins
  • Repeatable merchant onboarding

We do not claim payment GMV, recurring revenue or verified paying customers.

The distinction is intentional: functional QA supports a beta-stage claim, not a transaction-volume or customer-retention claim.

04 / Why this business can scale

Sell a reusable merchant layer, not one-off websites.

The equity thesis depends on proving that one product can be adopted repeatedly at a sustainable merchant acquisition cost.

Low migration friction

Oído is designed to sit alongside existing payment and operations tools. Its initial purchase is a narrower decision than switching POS providers.

Recurring value, if verified

Monthly subscriptions would be justified by repeat guest usage, ordering, tips, feedback and reporting — not simply the creation of a menu page.

06 / Go-to-market

Prove the economics locally. Then test GCC.

Oído is Barcelona-based and not yet incorporated. The UAE/GCC is a planned first international validation market, not a claimed operating footprint.

Validate the buyer

Interview independent cafés, restaurants and bars; identify a narrow first segment and concrete switching objections.

Run measurable pilots

Complete payment-provider readiness, onboard initial merchants and measure activation, willingness to pay, usage and support load.

Test the GCC case

Use UAE operator conversations and local partnerships to verify distribution and payments requirements before expanding operations.

No incorporation, live UAE operations, payment GMV, paying pilot count or commercial partnership is implied by this roadmap.

07 / Competitive context

Compete on adoption friction — not POS feature count.

Restaurant tills and payment suites remain the operational backbone. The proposed wedge is a lighter guest-facing layer, designed to work beside them.

Future options under exploration: AR menus and launch tools

Dish view · AR

Guests see a dish at real size on their own table through the phone camera, from the venue’s photos, and add it to the order from the same view. A possible future Venue capability, intended to support average order and fewer “what is this?” questions to staff.

Launch kit · new owners

For businesses that are just opening: a ready website, Google and booking listings, Instagram posts with autoposting, delivery and catering listings, a closed circle of owners by sector, and referrals. Exploratory concept, subject to merchant demand and tests of acquisition economics.

Invest in the proof between functional beta and repeatable merchant economics.

What capital would support

No production revenue, GMV or signed commercial contracts are presented as established facts. The next financing milestone is merchant and unit-economics evidence, not feature count alone.
