# Oído validation plan

Oído is currently a working browser beta and pilot proposition. The next step is not more surface area; it is external evidence that merchants and guests repeatedly use the product and that the operating model is supportable.

## Evidence ladder

1. **Problem evidence** — merchant can describe the current service/menu/payment friction without being led to the answer.
2. **Setup evidence** — a merchant can publish a usable Oído experience without replacing the existing POS or requiring bespoke implementation.
3. **Activation evidence** — real guests reach the service/menu flow and complete the intended customer action.
4. **Repeat-use evidence** — the merchant keeps Oído active after the novelty/pilot period.
5. **Willingness-to-pay evidence** — a merchant agrees to a paid commercial term rather than only expressing interest.
6. **Operating evidence** — support load, edits, refunds/payment questions and staff onboarding remain manageable.

## What to measure in pilots

- merchant segment and current stack;
- time from agreement to a usable live experience;
- staff setup/training effort;
- customer scans / sessions and completed intended actions;
- repeat merchant usage by week;
- merchant-requested changes and support minutes;
- reasons for abandonment or non-use;
- stated vs accepted commercial terms;
- payment-provider and POS integration blockers.

## Decision rules

A pilot is not counted as commercial validation because someone liked the demo. Evidence should move through the ladder above.

Do **not** claim GMV, payment conversion or revenue before licensed settlement is live and reconciled.

Do **not** silently redefine activation after seeing the data. Define the intended merchant and guest action before each pilot.

## Current boundary

The website, investor view, responsive browser tests and pilot enquiry flow are real. Paid merchant demand, retention, GMV and revenue remain unproven and are labelled that way in the public materials.
